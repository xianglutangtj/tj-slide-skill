# Story and wording

## The audience

Assume the audience knows the field but not the structure in the speaker's head. Do not teach from zero; make the hidden organisation visible. They want to know: where are we, why, what was missing, what happens next, and who does it.

## Deck plan (write this first)

```markdown
# <Deck name> — plan
- Audience:
- When I finish, they should:
- Time / setting:
- Source material, and what in it is final:

## Slides
1. <Claim as a sentence>
   - Visual: <timeline + process with gaps + three-box summary>
   - Clicks: timeline → process → solid → not solid → so
2. …

## Left out on purpose
- <item> — <why: other audience / repeats slide 1 / goes in notes>
```

The "left out" list matters as much as the slide list: it is how the user sees what was cut and can ask for something back.

## Story shapes

**Status or progress update (default, 3 slides)**

1. *Where we are, and why.* A short timeline, then the usual process with done and skipped steps marked, then the three-box summary.
2. *What happens now.* One loop that holds every open item: step → step → check → "all good?" → onward, or back to step 1.
3. *Who does what.* One card per person; one strip for anything on hold, with its one reason.

**Teaching a framework or figure (about 5 slides)**

1. Starting idea → current version, and the one question that links them.
2. What the evidence is and where its boundary lies — only the numbers needed for credibility.
3. What was looked at or coded, as one concrete question.
4. Read the figure one layer at a time: broad → specific → weight → outcome.
5. Today's focus, and the handoff to the next speaker.

**Asking for a decision (2–3 slides)**

1. The decision as a question, and why it is needed now.
2. The options side by side, same attributes for each.
3. The recommendation and what each person does if it is accepted.

Change the count when the argument needs it. Add a slide only when a new kind of picture must be taught. Merge slides that answer the same question.

## Cutting

For every item ask, in this order:

1. Is it for **this** audience? If not, delete it.
2. Is it already on another slide? If so, delete one.
3. Could the speaker simply say it? Then it goes to the notes.
4. Is it a method detail that does not change the claim? Notes or appendix.
5. Does the audience need the source label to trust it? If not, drop the label.

What survives is drawn, not listed.

## Wording

- **Titles are claims.** "The coding scheme is a first draft: two review steps were skipped", not "Current status".
- **Phrases on the slide.** "Check sample cases, revise the rules", not "The reviewer will check a sample of cases and then revise the rules accordingly."
- **Keep a sentence only when it is irreplaceable** — a question the audience must answer, or a boundary that a phrase would blur ("Not confirmed by the two of us yet.").
- **Plain words.** Prefer "sort the papers again" to "re-run classification"; "categories" to "taxonomy" unless the audience says taxonomy.
- **Process language.** Verbs first, in order: set → try → revise → run → check.
- **People, not passive voice.** Every step names who does it.
- **Numbers carry their unit**: "120 cases", never a bare "120" and never a different unit implied.
- **One name per thing** across slides, notes and file names.
- **Define on arrival** any term the audience may not share, in three or four words.

Rough budget: a title of at most 12 words; at most about 40 words appearing on any one click; a full slide after its last click under about 90 words excluding the title.

## The three-box summary

Close each part that reviews a state of affairs with three boxes that appear one click at a time:

| Box | Answers | Example |
|---|---|---|
| What is solid | What can the audience rely on? | Finding and screening papers |
| What is not | What has not been checked or agreed? | The categories, and where each paper sits |
| So | What follows for us? | This part needs more work from us |

Each box is one phrase. The reasons are spoken.

## New versus carried over

When a recent meeting added requests, tag them **New** inside the workflow where they belong, with a one-line legend. Things promised earlier and not yet done get one plain line — what, and the one reason — rather than a slide of their own.

## Speaker notes

One short paragraph per slide, written as "Click 1: … Click 2: …". Each click's note says what the audience is looking at, the point to make, and any number or caveat that was kept off the slide. The click count in the notes must equal the click count of the slide.

For timing, 130–150 spoken words per minute is a reasonable estimate. Call it an estimate until it has been rehearsed aloud.
